Vocabulary Builder: 14 Key Expressions

Explore 14 essential expressions and idioms categorized for professional and general communication. Click on any card to reveal its definition and contextual examples.

#1 PIECE OF CAKE

MeaningExample
Something that is easily achieved.I said that this class would be a piece of cake.
An effortless task.Cooking is a piece of cake for my friend.
Simple and manageable.Making rice is a piece of cake for some people, but hard for others.

#2 BEAR IN MIND

MeaningExample
Remember and take into account.She needs to bear in mind that the figures vary from place to place.
Be mindful of.Just bear in mind that you are not alone.
To remember something when making decisions.Bearing in mind that he’s had so little experience, I thought he did a good job.

#3 HAVE IT DOWN PAT

MeaningExample
To know or be able to do something perfectly.I have to memorize these words until I have them down pat.
Be a perfect master of something.He had his story down pat.
To learn something so well it requires no thought.Samantha had her answers down pat, but she knew they didn’t believe her.

#4 IN ONE EAR AND OUT THE OTHER

MeaningExample
Heard but disregarded or quickly forgotten.Everything the teacher told her students went in one ear and out the other.
Failure to retain information.I told him, but I think it went in one ear and out the other.
Information is ignored immediately.The woman felt that everything she told her husband went in one ear and out the other.

#5 RING A BELL

MeaningExample
To revive a distant recollection.The name Michael rings a bell, but I’m not 100% sure I remember him.
To sound familiar.I’ve never met Sally, but her name rings a bell.
For something to cause someone to remember something.I’m sorry, that description doesn’t ring a bell.

#6 CHIME IN

MeaningExample
To interject a remark.“Yes, you do that,” Donna chimed in.
To join in harmoniously (with a suggestion).The new employee chimed in with a suggestion.
To enter a conversation, often spontaneously.Everyone in the room began to chime in with their own ideas.

#7 AS FIT AS A FIDDLE

MeaningExample
To be in good health.If you drink this tea, you’ll be fit as a fiddle tomorrow.
Robust and healthy.My grandfather said that he feels as fit as a fiddle.
Perfectly well.The doctor was fit as a fiddle.

#8 MAP SOMETHING OUT

MeaningExample
To plan something in detail.The department wrote a new document mapping out its policies.
To determine a path or future course.Her future is all mapped out ahead of her.
To devise a complete plan.I will map out everything for you.

#9 FALL OFF THE MAP

MeaningExample
To disappear from view and attention.She fell off of the map.
Drop out of sight (lose fame/relevance).The actor used to be famous, but he fell off the map about a year ago.
To stop being known or considered.That team played in the championship and then they fell off the map.

#10 PUT SOMETHING ON THE MAP

MeaningExample
To make some place, product, or person famous or popular.The good desserts they serve there will really put them on the map.
To elevate status or recognition.This once boring town is now on the map.
To create significant renown.The iPod put Apple on the map.

#11 BIRTHDAY SUIT

MeaningExample
Naked or nude.Mike embarrassed his grandmother by answering the door in his birthday suit.
State of being unclothed.She started to blush when she saw him in his birthday suit.
Humorous reference to being naked.He made a joke that he would go to the party in his birthday suit.

#12 PUT ON YOUR THINKING CAP

MeaningExample
Meditate on a problem or think hard.The teacher told her students to put their thinking caps on.
To think seriously about something.Do you have your thinking cap on?
To prepare to solve a problem.I told my niece to put her thinking cap on.

#13 OFF-THE-CUFF

MeaningExample
Without preparation (impromptu).The student decided to do his speech off-the-cuff.
Spoken or done without rehearsal.Sometimes people speak off-the-cuff, but it doesn’t always turn out well.
Improvisational.He spoke off-the-cuff but it was amazing.

#14 COERCE

MeaningExample
To compel by force or intimidation.He tried to coerce him into opposing the decision, but his efforts failed.
To compel by authority (Pressurize).Do you think they have the power to coerce China?
To persuade someone to do something (usually by force).The North tried to coerce the South to agree with them.
